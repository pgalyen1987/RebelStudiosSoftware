Remove EXIF Metadata From a Photo

The hidden GPS and camera data your photos carry — and how to strip it

August 10, 2026 Patrick Galyen

Every photo your phone takes carries a hidden file called EXIF metadata: the exact GPS coordinates where it was shot, the device model, the date and time, and camera settings. It travels with the image. When you post that photo to a marketplace, a forum, a dating profile, or email it to a stranger, you may be handing over where you live.

It’s one of the most common accidental privacy leaks online — and it’s easy to prevent.

Why It Matters

The GPS tag is the dangerous one. A photo of an item for sale, taken in your living room, can pin your home address to within a few meters. Selling something to a stranger, posting where you live, or sending an ID photo to an unknown portal are all moments where you don’t want your coordinates riding along.

Many big social networks strip metadata on upload — but many marketplaces, forums, email attachments, and file-sharing links do not. Don’t assume; strip it yourself.

Free Ways to Strip It

  • iPhone: in the share sheet, tap Options at the top and turn Location off before you share. That removes GPS from the shared copy.
  • Windows: right-click the file → Properties → Details tab → Remove Properties and Personal Information → create a copy with the data removed.
  • Mac: in Photos, select the image → Image menu → Location → Hide/Remove Location; or open in Preview and use the Inspector to strip GPS.
  • Quick and dirty: screenshot the photo. A screenshot has none of the original EXIF — though you lose a little quality and the true resolution.

These work well for a single image when you remember to do them. The failure mode is forgetting, or having a batch to clean.

FAQ

Does stripping metadata change the image?

No — the pixels are untouched. Only the hidden EXIF data (GPS, camera info, timestamps) is removed.

What exactly gets removed?

GPS coordinates, device make/model, capture date/time, and camera settings — the fields that identify where and how the photo was taken.

Don’t social networks already do this?

Some do on upload, but many marketplaces, forums, email attachments, and direct file links preserve it. When in doubt, strip it before you share.
