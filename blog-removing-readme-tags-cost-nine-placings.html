Removing five readme tags cost us nine search placings

The words were all still in the readme bodies. Only the Tags field changed, and every phrase that lost a slot fell within seven hours.

November 25, 2026 Rebel Studios Software

Six days ago I published a post here saying that one readme tag took one of our plugins from absent to fourth in ten hours, and I ended it by pointing at the hole in my own experiment: the commit that swapped the tag had also added a sentence to the description, so the tag was not the only thing that changed.

On 24 November we ran the other half by accident. Two of our WordPress.org plugins had their five tags replaced, and this time I had a clean before: the rank timer had logged every phrase at 09:44 UTC, ten hours before the first of the two commits landed. Here is what came back.

What actually changed

Two readme.txt commits, one per plugin. No PHP, no version bump, no release.

# restaurant menu plugin, 19:11 UTC
-Tags: allergen menu, deli, bistro, pizzeria, brewery
+Tags: restaurant menu, qr code menu, table reservations, food menu, allergen menu

# membership plugin, 20:12 UTC
-Tags: alumni directory, congregation, society members, church members, club membership
+Tags: membership, content restriction, restrict content, member directory, members only

Each commit touched more than its tag line — the restaurant one reworded a bullet and added two FAQ answers, the membership one rewrote its opening paragraph and added two more — and I will come back to that, because one of those edits turns out to matter more than any of the tags did.

The important thing about the swap is what it did not remove. Every word in both deleted tag lines is still in the live readme body: deli, bistro, pizzeria and brewery each appear once in the restaurant plugin’s description, and alumni, church, club, society, congregation, membership, directory and members all appear in the membership plugin’s. Directory search ANDs the words in your query, so none of those phrases went missing from the text. Each one simply moved from tagged and written to written only.

Nine for nine, in fields of fourteen to ninety-nine plugins. Those were not placings we were given; they were earned on 21 and 22 November by putting those words in the readme in the first place.

The part that makes it a measurement: what held

The same sweep covered twenty-odd phrases that did not lose a tag slot, and they are the reason I am willing to say the tag field did this rather than the directory having a quiet Tuesday.

Fourteen were identical before and after: catering menu, coffee shop menu, contactless menu, dairy free menu, dietary tags, gluten free menu, pub menu, vegan menu, vegetarian menu, wine list, winery, hoa members, union members and table reservation — the same mixture of venue words and dietary words as the ones that fell, all of them body-only, none of them moving a place. allergen menu, the single tag that survived the swap, held 12th of 66.

Drift is real but small, and I would rather give you the size of it than pretend it is zero: diner menu improved by one, gym members lost one, and member accent color — a phrase nobody has ever optimised, on two different plugins — lost one place on one and two on the other. So I am not claiming the four single-place drops in that table individually. The claim rests on −3, −3, −4, −7 and −8, which are outside anything the controls did, and on the direction being nine out of nine.

One control moved more than two places, and it is the interesting one: printable qr code took the restaurant plugin from 43rd to 47th of 151. That phrase kept its body mention and never had a tag — but it is also the exact wording the restaurant commit changed, from “printable QR menu” to “printable QR code menu”. An edit meant to help it is sitting next to a four-place fall. I cannot attribute that one, and the directory’s ranking is not documented, so I am leaving it on the table rather than fitting a story to it.

Measured 2026-11-25 07:19 UTC, twelve hours after the tags went live. I will not call table reservations a gain: it has no baseline, nobody measured it before the swap, and its singular form — same field size, in the history file since 20 November — sat at 85th before the swap and 85th after.

This is the second time we have measured that, in the second direction. Four of those words were tags on 22 November, were measured absent, and were removed for being absent. Putting four of them back for twelve hours changed nothing: all four are still absent from the top hundred. A wall-sized phrase in your Tags field does not get you into the wall.

One sentence of body text did what five tags could not

The membership plugin appeared in two fields on this run that it had never appeared in before: allergen menu at 48th of 66, and printable qr code at 71st of 151. Neither phrase is one of its tags, and neither has anything to do with what it does.

They came from one line in the same commit. Its readme carries a short “Other plugins by Rebel Studios” list, and that commit repointed those links at wordpress.org and retitled one entry:

-* Restaurant Menu & Reservations: a menu, table reservations and QR ordering.
+* Restaurant Menu & Reservations: a menu with allergen labels, table
+  reservations and a printable QR code menu.

That is the whole mechanism. Eight words of ordinary prose, written to fix a cross-link, put a membership plugin into two restaurant searches on the same day that five of the biggest words in its category, sitting in the field designed for search terms, put it into none.

It is a misfire, not a win — the wrong plugin in the right field, and nobody searching for an allergen menu wants a membership plugin. But it is the cleanest thing in the run: the body is where the directory reads from, and the Tags field behaves like a small multiplier on a field you can already reach rather than a ticket into one you cannot.

So what are five tag slots worth

Three things we are taking from this, and the third is the one I did not expect.

A tag earns you places, not fields. Both measurements now point the same way: adding a tag for a phrase you already write about moves you up a small field, and adding one for a phrase with two thousand competitors does nothing at all. Nine mid-tail placings fell when their tags went; nine high-volume tags arrived and bought one unbaselined 65th.

Five slots is a position, not a form to fill in. This is the bit worth having before you edit. The instinct with tags is to treat them as free guesses — type the five biggest words and see what happens. But once a tag is earning you a placing, the slot is a thing you are holding, and swapping it is a sale. We paid nine placings for one.

It is cheap enough to be worth measuring and small enough to stay honest about. All four of our directory plugins report zero active installs and zero ratings as I write this, which the directory shows as nothing at all. WordPress.org ranks on relevance and popularity together, so what is available to a plugin with no installs is a placing in a field of fourteen. Sixth of fourteen rather than ninth of fourteen is not traffic. It is the one lever that is free, reversible, and lands inside a day, and that is the entire reason to bother with it.

Nothing is restored as I write this. The evidence says put both old tag lines back and spend one freed slot on table reservations, which at least placed — but a measurement that reverses somebody’s deliberate edit is a conversation, not a script’s decision.

If you want to do this to your own plugin

You need a before, and the awkward thing about a before is that you need it in place on the day you had no reason to want one. Ours exists because a rank check runs on a timer and appends one record per run to a file, so the baseline for this experiment was ten hours old and nobody had to remember to take it. The whole thing is one API endpoint — api.wordpress.org/plugins/info/1.2/ with action=query_plugins and your phrase — and the only two traps worth passing on are that the square brackets in request[search] have to be percent-encoded or you get an empty body that reads exactly like “no results”, and that you should record the field size and your competitors’ install counts next to your rank, because a rank on its own cannot tell you whether you lost to a better listing or to a plugin with ten thousand installs and ten years in the directory.

Two plugins, eleven phrases, one directory, measured over seventeen hours: that is the whole sample, and the mechanism behind the ranking is not published. Treat it as what it is — a before and an after on our own listings, with the controls shown so you can see how much of it is weather.

The earlier half of this experiment, including the confound this one fixes, is here: One readme tag took us from unfindable to fourth in ten hours. And if you are about to edit a readme and expect the page to change, read wordpress.org reads your readme from the stable tag, not trunk first — we lost a day to that one.
