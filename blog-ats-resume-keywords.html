ATS Resume Keywords

Why resumes get auto-rejected before a human ever sees them

August 10, 2026 Patrick Galyen

Two hundred applications. A handful of rejections, mostly silence. It feels personal, but a lot of the time no person was involved: an applicant tracking system (ATS) parsed your resume into fields, scored it against the job, and filed it below the cutoff before a recruiter opened it.

The good news is that most ATS failures aren’t about your experience — they’re about formatting and keywords, and both are fixable in an afternoon.

What an ATS Actually Does

An ATS ingests your file, tries to pull out structured data (name, titles, dates, skills), and matches it against the job description. If it can’t parse a section, that section effectively doesn’t exist. If the skills the posting asks for don’t appear in words it recognizes, you look unqualified even when you’re not.

Fix the Formatting First (Free)

Before keywords, make sure the machine can read you at all:

  • No columns, tables, or text boxes. Multi-column layouts scramble when parsed — your bullet points can end up interleaved with your job titles. Use a single-column layout.
  • No images, icons, or logos for anything that carries meaning. An ATS can’t read text baked into a graphic.
  • Standard section headers it recognizes: “Experience,” “Education,” “Skills.” Cute headers like “Where I’ve Made an Impact” get ignored.
  • A normal font and simple bullet characters.
  • Save as a text-based file — a normal .docx, or a PDF exported from text (not a scanned or image-exported PDF). If the ATS can’t select the text, it can’t read it.

Then Match the Keywords

This is where most people lose points. The ATS looks for the specific terms in the posting, and it is dumb about synonyms:

  • Mirror the posting’s exact words. If it says “Kubernetes,” don’t only write “k8s.” If it says “accounts receivable,” don’t only write “AR.” Include the full term at least once.
  • Pull the hard skills, tools, and titles straight from the job description and make sure each relevant one appears naturally in your experience or skills section.
  • Spell out acronyms once (“Search Engine Optimization (SEO)”) so you match whichever form the posting uses.
  • Don’t keyword-stuff. A hidden wall of terms or white-on-white text gets flagged and read by a human later — it backfires. Weave the terms into real accomplishments.

FAQ

Can an ATS read a PDF?

A text-based PDF, yes. A PDF that’s really an image (scanned or exported as a picture) often can’t be parsed. If you can’t select the text with your cursor, neither can the ATS — use a .docx or a proper text PDF.

Is keyword stuffing worth the risk?

No. It may squeak past the parser, but a recruiter sees it and it reads as desperate or dishonest. Match the real terms honestly.

Do I need a fancy template?

The opposite. Simple, single-column, standard-header layouts parse best. Design flourishes are what break ATS parsing.

The Takeaway

Most “no responses” are a formatting-and-keywords problem, not a you problem. Clean up the layout, mirror the posting’s exact terms, and save a text-based file — that’s 80% of it, free.
